Is the corrected real scatter physical, and is it the right quantity?
The exceedance rests on a corrected real-scatter distribution reaching p95 about 8 voxels against a winding spacing of about 12.81. This checks that figure without reusing the correction model, and then asks whether the quantity is the one the exceedance needs.

=== The raw geometry, which is the part worth keeping ===
  Deviation from a plane, no surrogate and no attenuation applied, pooled across patches.
   window    real extent (vox)     rms p50     p95      max        n
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
   3x4       60 x 80          0.735    2.025     5.25      209
   5x6      100 x 120         1.897    4.540     6.90      209
   9x12     180 x 240         5.656   12.132    25.18      207

=== The concern is NOT closed ===
  The corrected p95 under scrutiny is 8.24 voxels. It is not an independent quantity: it IS the analysis-window plane-fit residual, divided by the attenuation. So it describes deviation inside a 60x80 voxel window.
  Directly observed deviation at that SAME window: p95 2.025.
  Gap: 4.1x, unexplained by anything measured here.
  An earlier version answered this by pointing at the largest window's p95, over a nine times larger area. That is a different quantity, and the comparison is a category error. It is also unstable: with the largest window at 7x9 rather than 9x12 the same argument fails outright. The concern stands.

=== And the shared-vs-local statistic has no power ===
  Observed on real data: local as a fraction of total, p50 0.846.
  Calibrated against fields of KNOWN composition, the same statistic reports:
     true local amplitude fraction | statistic reports
    ----------------------------------------------
                             0.02 |             0.383
                             0.05 |             0.495
                             0.10 |             0.698
                             0.20 |             0.905
                             0.50 |             0.978
  It saturates, and steeply. Inverting the curve at the observed value puts the TRUE local fraction between 0.10 and 0.20 -- not at the observed number itself. Reading 0.85 as 'about 85 percent genuinely perturbs' inverts the answer: most of the deviation is shared curvature after all.
  So the branch this probe carried for 'most of it is shared curvature, therefore the exceedance is an overestimate' did not fail to fire. It could not fire, and its silence was not evidence. On this calibration it should have fired.
  Note also that the split is not a partition: 35.7 percent of samples have local exceeding total, which an orthogonal decomposition cannot do. 'X percent of the deviation' has no variance interpretation here.

  Both halves of the earlier closure are withdrawn. The exceedance still rests on a corrected distribution roughly 4x larger than anything observed at its own scale, and whether that is physical is unresolved. Resolving it needs the attenuation validated against a quantity measured at the analysis window, not at a larger one.
