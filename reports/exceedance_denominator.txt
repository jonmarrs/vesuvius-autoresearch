What is in the denominator of the published exceedance?

About 46% of rays produce no divergence threshold, and the exceedance counts
them as non-exceedances. Two very different things were being counted together:
a ray whose correctly placed patch already fails at zero scatter was never
eligible for the test, while a ray whose reference passes and whose verdicts
still never differ is a genuine case of the metric not noticing.

  rays: 40, seeds: 3, surrogate 1.2/1.0

   class        share    meaning
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
   degenerate    0.0%    reference already unsatisfied at zero scatter
   immune       50.8%    reference passes, verdicts never differ
   diverges     49.2%    verdicts differ somewhere on the ladder

  Degenerate rays are 0.0%, within the pre-registered 10% limit. The published denominator is sound: the
  no-onset rays are genuine immunity, which is exactly what a non-exceedance
  should mean, and 23.59% stands as computed.

  ⚠ But be clear about how much of that was ever in doubt. `build_synthetic_patch`
  places the patch EXACTLY on a winding, so at zero scatter the reference scores a
  satisfied-quad fraction of exactly 1.0 on all 40 rays against a 0.95 threshold -- measured, not
  assumed. The degenerate class is therefore empty by construction of the test
  patch rather than as a discovered property of the data, and this half of the
  rule could not have fired. The informative output is the immune/diverges split
  below it, which was not predetermined. A real traced patch could well fail at
  zero scatter; that question is not asked here and is not answered by this.

=== How robust is the immune fraction? ===
  The report leans on this one as the practical number that survives without the
  calibration, so it is worth knowing what it does depend on. Not attenuation --
  no corrected scatter enters it. But it IS a property of the injected field's
  shape and of how far the ladder reaches, and both are swept here.

   surrogate                              ladder to 4.0   ladder to 20.0
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
   white                                        88.3%            56.7%
   isotropic 0.561 (published)                  65.0%            52.5%
   isotropic 1.236                              50.8%            50.8%
   anisotropic 1.20 / 1.00 (admissible)         50.8%            48.3%

  Across surrogates, at the wider ladder, immunity spans 48.3% to 56.7%, a spread of 8.3%.
  Within 10 points, so the figure is a property of the metric rather than of
  which field is injected, and quoting one number for it is fair.
  Extending the ladder can only lower immunity, since it offers more chances to
  flip, so the wide column is a lower bound and the narrow one is not the answer.

This says nothing about whether the corrected scatter is right, which is the k question examined elsewhere. It asks only what population the average is over.
