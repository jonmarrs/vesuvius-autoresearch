What moves the exceedance: correlation magnitude, not anisotropy
This probe originally concluded that fitting a surrogate to both measured axis statistics raised the exceedance 2.6x, and attributed that to anisotropy. Review showed the comparison confounded the axis RATIO with the correlation MAGNITUDE: the anisotropic arm smooths both axes far harder than the published isotropic one. Holding effective sample size fixed and varying only the ratio separates them.

=== The two statistics, and the pipeline artifact behind the negative one ===
  real across-column lag-1 (axis 1): +0.357
  real across-row    lag-1 (axis 0): -0.076
  the SAME pipeline on pure white noise:    column -0.327, row -0.252
  A plane fit over three rows with three parameters induces negative row correlation on ANY field, so the real -0.076 indicates POSITIVE intrinsic row correlation partly cancelling that artifact, not anti-correlated data. Reasoning from the sign alone sent a first attempt after a high-pass filter, which converged to its boundary.

  fitted sigma_col = 1.450, sigma_row = 1.050 (joint 2-D search, residual cost 0.008)
  achieved: across-column +0.365 (target +0.357), across-row -0.047 (target -0.076)

=== Three arms, with a magnitude-only control ===
  Effective sample size on the 12x16 injection grid (1/Var of the grid mean; white noise would be the cell count):
    A isotropic (published fit)        sigma (0.561, 0.561)   ESS   60.66
    B isotropic (ESS-matched control)  sigma (1.236, 1.236)   ESS   12.61
    C anisotropic (both axes fitted)   sigma (1.450, 1.050)   ESS   12.61

  Exceedance, mean +- sd over 8 seeds, same rays and same real scatter:
    arm                                 rays flipping   exceedance
    ----------------------------------------------------------
    A isotropic (published fit)          15.1 of 40    2.86% +- 0.35
    B isotropic (ESS-matched control)    23.0 of 40    6.75% +- 0.44
    C anisotropic (both axes fitted)     23.4 of 40    6.79% +- 0.51

  A to C (originally claimed for anisotropy): x2.38
  A to B (MAGNITUDE alone, ratio fixed at 1): x2.36
  B to C (ANISOTROPY alone, ESS fixed):       x1.01
  => anisotropy accounts for about 1% of the log-effect. The mover is total correlation.

  Why the magnitude changed at all: the two arms were fitted to DIFFERENT statistics. The published surrogate fitted lag-1 on the RAW field; this one fits lag-1 on the PLANE-FIT RESIDUAL, which is how the real target was measured. That, not the axis count, moved sigma from 0.56 to about 1.45. Correcting the fitting criterion is the real finding here.

=== What this does NOT settle ===
  The exceedance divides real reported scatter by CAL_K = 0.602, an attenuation fitted under the OLD isotropic surrogate. Refitting it under this one gives k near 0.257, which would put corrected real scatter near 3.2 voxels rather than 1.33 and push every exceedance above well past what is quoted. So these figures are a hybrid of two incompatible surrogates and belong to neither. Flagged rather than resolved.
  Also unresolved: lag-1 on two axes is two numbers describing a 2-D field, and an isotropic field CAN reach the column target alone at sigma about 3.5 -- so the isotropic family does not bound this from below. These arms widen a bracket; none supersedes the others.
