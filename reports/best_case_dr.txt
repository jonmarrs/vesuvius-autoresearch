Is a real window unsatisfiable, or was it scored at the wrong dr?

The previous probe scored every real window at the published median dr of
12.81 voxels and found 21.7% satisfied. Real spacings run 11.32 to 16.74,
so this gives each window the dr that suits it best and asks whether it can be
satisfied at all. The result is an UPPER BOUND by construction: no fitter can
pick dr to maximise its own score without already knowing the answer.

=== Verdict on the pre-registered rule ===
  ⚠ Only 48.3% of real windows are satisfied at ANY spacing in the physical range, below the pre-registered 50%.
  BUT the margin is 1.7% against a standard error of 6.5% on n=60, so this verdict is inside the noise: the data cannot
  distinguish this share from the threshold, and the rule firing 'below'
  is not evidence that it is below. Treat the direction as unresolved and
  the doubling below as the real result.

  What is NOT marginal: allowing each window a physical spacing that suits it takes the satisfied share from 21.7% to 48.3%, more than double. The previous probe's 21.7% was
  measured against one global constant and understates the metric on real
  geometry by about that factor. The quad-matched windows are unaffected: 0% at
  every spacing tried.

The whole-winding delta is re-checked at each window's own best dr, not at a shared constant, so the real-data blindness result does not depend on which dr the previous probe happened to use.
